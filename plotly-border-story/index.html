What truck crossings can tell us about the border

A public-data story shaped by building and operating BorderPulse.

A crossing count answers a specific question: how many trucks entered the United States through a port in a given month? Comparing those counts can reveal differences in volume over time. Explaining what changed requires more evidence.

Read the measure before the trend

In the saved BTS extract, Otay Mesa recorded 990,111 truck entries in 2025, down 6.6% from 1,059,759 in 2024. That is a change in reported crossings. The counts alone cannot tell us whether cargo value, shipping demand or waiting times changed.

For a logistics researcher, that difference is a reason to investigate. The monthly view shows whether the change is concentrated in a few months or spread across the year. The source records let a colleague check the observation before using it in a brief.

The selected measure is Trucks, not loaded containers, cargo value or unique vehicles. These are inbound counts; they do not describe both directions of travel. Month lengths also differ, and these counts are not adjusted for that difference.

Port boundaries matter. El Paso and Ysleta are separate records in this dataset. Combining them without saying so would change the question. Missing observations remain missing; a reported zero stays zero.

How this sample was made

Created for Plotly’s content and web team as a reusable editorial example: one focused question, an inspectable chart and source records readers can take with them. No internal Plotly data or commercial performance claims are used.

BTS dataset keg4-3bc2 was retrieved November 6, 2026. The sample includes 165 port-month records for five ports, January 2024–September 2026. Records were filtered to Trucks and the U.S.–Mexico border, then checked for duplicate port-month keys and nonnegative integer counts. Period totals are sums of displayed records; missing periods do not receive a complete total.

Built with Plotly.js 4.1.1. Sebastian chose the concept, reviewed the first working version and directed this refinement. OpenAI Codex assisted with research, copy, implementation and checks. The underlying work experience is Sebastian’s; AI assistance does not imply unaided implementation. There is no live AI generation or claimed Plotly Studio/Dash deployment.
