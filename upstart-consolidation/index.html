The monthly payment matters. So does having enough to pay off your debt—and knowing how long you’ll be paying.

  1. 01

    Look at the money you receive

    An upfront fee may come out before funds are sent. A $10,000 loan with an $815 deducted fee leaves $9,185 to use.

  2. 02

    Check what remains unpaid

    Compare that available cash with your current payoff balance. A gap remains debt you would still need to address.

  3. 03

    Read monthly and total payments together

    More payments can mean a smaller monthly amount and a larger total. Neither number alone tells the full story.

Behind this work sample

The hypothesis, sources, and production decisions.

Why this experience

Upstart’s personal-loan page includes a monthly-payment calculator and explains deducted origination fees separately. This independent concept brings the cash available, payoff gap, and scheduled repayment tradeoff into one pre-application learning experience.

Hypothesis: clearer net-proceeds information could improve borrower comprehension and the quality of a later application decision. No conversion lift, customer demand, or ROI has been measured.

This is marketing-site work, not a redesign of Upstart’s authenticated offer or underwriting experience. It has no affiliation with or endorsement by Upstart.

What a useful test would measure

First test whether participants can correctly identify the money received, remaining debt, and payment tradeoff. Only then consider an approved experiment measuring informed continuation and completed applications, with confusion and abandonment as guardrails.

Variant A leads with the net proceeds. Variant B leads with scheduled monthly and total payments. All calculations and disclosures remain the same. There is no automatic assignment or live experiment.
