What the studies show

Learning patterns

MitoSpace learns from mitochondrial shape and movement in three-dimensional microscopy over time. In this study, adding spatial and temporal information improved the learned representations.

One demonstrated use was predicting mitochondrial membrane potential, an energy-related measurement.

Read the MitoSpace abstract

Modeling physical behavior

A separate project uses a physics-based digital twin to represent selected cellular structures and processes. It models how those parts may move and interact.

This is a different approach from MitoSpace’s pattern learning. It is also separate from the illustration on this page.

Read the digital-twin abstract

Combining the approaches, and their possible role in drug discovery, is future work.

Sources and limits

Source check: October 28, 2026. The explanations draw on the university’s September 17 report and the two paper abstracts. Full experimental methods and independent replication were not reviewed.

  1. 4D spatiotemporal landscape of mitochondrial phenotypes across cellular states unlocked through representation learning. Cell, September 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.028.
  2. Whole-cell particle-based digital twin simulations from 4D lattice light-sheet microscopy data. Cell, July 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.06.010.

The original schematic contains no study data, microscopy, research code, model output, physical units or drug-response predictions. A and B are illustrative tracking positions. Colors distinguish forms; they do not encode measured biological properties. The invented motion is not a reproduction of mitochondrial dynamics.

The research findings are not clinical diagnoses. Neither the research nor this sample is presented as a complete biochemical cell or a patient-specific treatment simulator. No scientific peer review of this sample has been performed.

How it was made

Sebastian directs the experience and makes release decisions. ChatGPT (Codex) assists research, editorial design and development.

The visual uses original browser-rendered geometry with an SVG alternative. No research assets, model weights or patient data are used. The work demonstrates a new application sample and does not imply prior professional scientific-modeling experience.

Private generated layout references guided the composition; they are not scientific imagery and are excluded from the website. The page uses locally served open-license Newsreader and Roboto fonts.

No analytics, forms, cookies or browser storage are used. Outbound research links lead to independently operated sites.

The intended institutional value is more relevant research and trainee inquiries. A production evaluation would compare visits to the lab’s contact and joining pages, then use the lab’s own inquiry records to assess relevance. Link clicks alone are not qualified leads. No conversion improvement has been measured.
